Six things that get you a real quote

Most enquiries that stall do so because the fabric was not described precisely enough to price. These six are the difference between a number and a range.

  1. Construction or reference. Fibre, blend, GSM, width, knit or woven. "Blue cotton" is not a specification; "100% cotton single jersey, 180 GSM, 58 in" is.
  2. Quantity in metres and kilograms, and the number of colourways. One colourway is one dye lot, and lots are never pooled. The last lot sets the date, not the first.
  3. Target shade. A physical swatch is the only reliable standard. A Pantone or TCX code is a starting point; it does not know which batch of greige it is going onto.
  4. Process route. What needs doing, and what has already been done. Sending pre-dyed fabric for printing is a different job from sending greige.
  5. Date, worked backwards. Tell us the delivery date and the destination, not just "urgent". We can tell you which stage is the constraint.
  6. Destination and certification. Which market it ships to and any certificate your buyer will demand. Our current position on every scheme is published on the quality page.
