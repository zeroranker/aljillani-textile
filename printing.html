Fabric printing in Faisalabad

Rotary screen, digital and flat screen printing are not interchangeable. Which one is right for your design is decided by colour count, repeat size, quantity and the effect you need, not by what the mill happens to own. This page sets out how each one behaves.

Choosing a method

Rotary, digital or flat screen

Send the artwork and we will tell you which method it should run on, and what it will cost at your quantity. If your design is a simple repeat in volume, rotary will almost always be cheaper per metre. If it has unlimited colours, no repeat, or a photographic element, it should not go near a rotary screen.

The digital-to-rotary move

A well-run programme samples digitally, proves the design, then moves to rotary once the volume justifies the engraving. It is the normal commercial path, and it is worth asking a printer to handle both stages.

If you do this, budget for a fresh strike-off. The same artwork renders differently between methods, different penetration, different hand, slightly different colour. A strike-off approved on digital does not automatically approve the rotary version.

The run

What happens between your file and a roll

  1. File and repeat check

    Resolution, colour space, repeat length and the number of colours. If the design has more colours than the method allows, this is where it is caught, not after the cylinders are cut.

  2. Pretreatment

    Print does not sit on raw greige. The fabric is prepared so the print has something to bond to, and a printing route on unprepared cloth is the most common cause of a print that cracks at the first fold.

  3. Strike-off

    A short length printed for approval. Typically one to two weeks to produce, subject to the print unit's queue. This is your chance to reject a colour, a registration or a hand before the bulk is committed.

    The clock starts after you approve this

  4. Bulk printing

    Each colourway runs as its own lot. On a rotary machine, each cylinder also carries its own repeat length, so a design change is a cylinder change, not a colour change.

  5. Steaming, washing and drying

    The print is fixed, then washed to remove unfixed pigment and excess thickener. A print that has not been properly washed smells, stiffens, and fades early.

  6. Finishing and inspection

    Usually the same stenter as the rest of the plant, which is why print programmes are seasonal, the finishing queue fills ahead of retail seasons.

Buyer questions

Printing questions worth asking

Digital or rotary: how do you decide?

On colour count, repeat size, quantity and effect. Below roughly a simple eight-to-twelve colour repeat in volume, rotary wins on cost per metre. Unlimited colours, no repeat, placement prints and photographic detail all belong on digital.

What is the minimum for a print run?

To confirmEDIT: the real figure, per colourway. Digital genuinely does open up small runs, and that is often the answer for a buyer who has been told no elsewhere.

Will a strike-off approved on digital carry over to rotary?

Not automatically. The same artwork renders differently between methods. A fresh strike-off is normal and should be budgeted for. See the method comparison above.

What file format and resolution do you need?

To confirmEDIT: state what you accept and what you need back. Most units work from vector artwork for rotary and high-resolution raster for digital. Sending the wrong one is the most common avoidable delay in a print enquiry.

Do you print and dye in the same order?

Sometimes, and the order matters. Printing onto undyed greige is not the same as printing after a dye, and a base colour changes what the print can achieve. Tell us the intended ground colour with your first enquiry rather than after the strike-off.
