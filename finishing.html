Fabric finishing

Finishing is where fabric stops being cloth and becomes a product. It is also where schedules are won and lost: almost every finish is another pass through the stenter, and the stenter is normally the bottleneck in the whole plant.

The bottleneck

Why the finishing route is a schedule, not a menu

Dyeing is a wet process that runs in parallel on many machines. Finishing mostly runs through one machine that everything else queues behind. A fabric that needs a softener, a peach finish and a raise does not get faster because you asked nicely, it gets three stenter passes and three places in the queue.

This is why we would rather agree the whole route at development than add a finish at the last minute. It is also why a quote for a "simple" job can be beaten by a mill with a longer route if the longer route queues better.

A useful question to ask any processor

"What is on the stenter today, and where does my lot sit in the queue?" A unit that answers with a date rather than a general statement is telling you something real.

Chemical finishes

These are applied on the stenter or a pad and change how the fabric behaves. Some of them are mutually exclusive, and a few quietly undo each other, which is the single most useful thing to know on this page.

The three questions that decide it

  1. Which of these does the end use genuinely need, and which was on the list because it always is?
  2. Is there a conflict between two of them (softener against wicking, for example)?
  3. How many stenter passes does the whole route add, and what is each pass waiting behind?

Buyer questions

Finishing questions worth asking

How much do finishes actually change the price?

More than most buyers expect, because every finish is a stenter pass and stenter time is the scarce resource. Two cheap finishes that must run in sequence can cost more than one expensive one that can run alone. Send us the full list at development and we will tell you which ones are free to add and which are not.

Can I have a soft fabric that still absorbs water?

Not easily. A softener reduces absorbency, which is why a soft towel and a soft t-shirt are opposite products. If absorbency matters, the softener has to be light or absent and the softness has to come from the mechanical route instead. Tell us the end use and we will propose a route that does not fight itself.

What shrinkage tolerance do you work to?

To confirmEDIT: state the tolerance you actually hold and the test method. Buyers in this trade commonly write GSM ±5 and width ±3 inches against the approved sample. If you work to a figure, publish it. It is a stronger claim than any certificate.

Can finishes be added to an order already in production?

Sometimes, and it depends entirely on where the lot is in the queue. The honest answer is usually "not without moving your date", because a new finish is a new stenter pass. Raise it before the dye lot is scheduled and the answer is usually yes.
