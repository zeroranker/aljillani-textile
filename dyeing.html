Before the dye

Pretreatment is where the shade is actually won

Dye that has not been properly cleaned out of the fabric will look correct on the day it comes off the stenter and will move after the first wash. This is the most common reason a cheap dye lot fails three months later, and it is a stage that can be skipped in a hurry to get a lot out of the door.

  1. Incoming inspection

    Weight, width, GSM and shade of the greige checked before it enters the process. Knitted and woven fabric needs different routes, and starting on the wrong one is not recoverable.

  2. Desize and scour: woven

    Size and weaving oils removed. Woven fabric arrives with both; neither is dye-friendly and both will affect the shade and the hand.

  3. Scour: knitted

    Knit fabric carries no size, but it carries spinning oil and machine oil. A caustic scour, not a desize, and the recipe depends on the yarn.

  4. Bleach: where required

    Peroxide bleaching, or hypochlorite for specific cases. White and pale shades need it; deep saturated shades generally do not, and bleaching a dark shade only weakens the fibre.

  5. Mercerise: where required

    Alkali treatment under tension. It increases dye uptake, gives lustre, and improves dimensional stability. On mercerised cotton a shade can be hit in one lot instead of two, which is often cheaper than the appearance of repeating the dye bath.

Why this matters to you

Ask any processor to quote a dye lot and the price will look similar across four or five units. The difference is what happened before the dye bath. Here is how to tell, without visiting:

  • Ask which pretreatment route they will use for your fabric, and why.
  • Ask whether mercerising is included or optional, and what it costs.
  • Ask what fastness they will guarantee, and against which test method.
  • Ask whether they retain your approved lab dip for repeat orders.

Shade matching

How a shade is agreed, and what it costs in time

Matching a shade is not a single step. It is a loop, and every round you reject restarts the production time as well as the waiting. This is why lab-dip turnaround is the number worth comparing between units.

1. Standard received
A physical swatch is the only reliable standard. A Pantone or TCX code is a starting point, not a target. A code does not know which lightbox your fabric will be judged in, or which batch of greige it is going onto.
2. Recipe built
Dye, salt, alkali, temperature, liquor ratio and time are set for your construction. Two fabrics of the same GSM can need different recipes; two shades of the same fabric usually do not.
3. Small-batch trial
Drawn on a sample machine (in this trade, typically 10–100 kg of capacity as against 200–1,400 kg on a bulk machine. That gap is why lab capacity is a real, separate resource and not a detail.
4. Matched and submitted
Compared under a standard light source, not daylight, and not on a phone screen. We submit options rather than a single attempt, because a single attempt is a coin toss on a new construction.
5. Your decision
Approved, rejected with notes, or approved with a tolerance. The clock stops here. We ask for a decision inside a stated window rather than holding a machine slot open.
6. Retained as the standard
The approved swatch is filed against your order. Repeat orders are re-matched against it, not against a fresh target. This is the single cheapest thing a processor can do for a buyer's brand risk, and the most commonly skipped.

Buyer questions

What buyers ask before placing a first order

What is your minimum order quantity?

If your quantity is below those numbers, say so in your first message. The answer is usually a different route rather than no.

How long does a lead time start from?

From shade approval, not from the purchase order. Published ranges in this trade are 30–60 days for dyeing after approval, and 20–40 days for printing after sample approval. If a unit quotes you 45 days "from order", ask what happens during the approval wait.

Both. We process fabric you supply, and we can also work on stocked greige. To confirmEDIT: confirm what is actually available and at what minimum.
