Quality, testing and what we actually hold

Every certificate in this trade can be verified with the body that issued it. So this page does two things: it explains how work is checked, and it states our position on every certification, including the ones we have not applied for.

Process control

How a lot is checked, at what points

Quality in a processing unit is not a final inspection department. It is a set of checks at the points where a fault becomes unrecoverable. Listed in the order they happen.

  1. Incoming greige

    Weight, width, GSM and shade of the fabric as it arrives. Anything outside the agreed tolerance is raised with you before it enters the process, not after the money is spent dyeing it.

  2. Recipe control

    Dye, salt, alkali, temperature, liquor ratio and time are set per construction, not per order. To confirmEDIT: state whether dosing is computerised.

  3. Lab dip

    Small-batch trial matched under a controlled light source. Options are submitted rather than a single attempt, because a single attempt is a coin toss on a new construction.

  4. Approved standard retained

    The approved swatch is filed against your order and used as the reference for the bulk and for every repeat. This is the single most valuable thing a processor can do for a buyer's brand risk, and the most commonly skipped.

    EDIT: confirm this is our policy and say so

  5. In-process shade

    Checked during the run, not only at the end. A lot that has drifted in the middle is corrected while it can still be corrected.

  6. Pre-dispatch inspection

    Bulk is not released without inspection against the retained approved sample. GSM, width, shade and faults are checked together, because a lot that passes three of the four and fails the fourth is still a rejected shipment.

Traceability: the thing that matters most in job work

A processor does not own its fabric, so the question a serious buyer asks is not "is your quality good" but "can you prove what went in and what came out". That is a records question, and records are the one thing a plant can do perfectly and almost nobody bothers to.

It is also the single most useful thing to get audited. The organic and recycled standards require exactly this, validating the scope and transaction certificates of incoming fabric, recording quantities purchased, and reconciling wastage and volume at every stage, and if those records already exist, certification stops being a rebuild and becomes a filing exercise.

To confirmEDIT: describe our own records honestly. Do not claim a system we do not run. Written by whoever actually keeps the batch records.

Input lot traceability

Every incoming lot recorded against its own reference, so a finished roll can be traced back to the greige it started from.

Batch records

Recipe, machine, operator, bath temperature curve and in-process shade check recorded per batch, and retained after the roll ships.

Volume reconciliation

Finished weight, process loss and wastage accounted for against input weight, per lot. This is the record an auditor asks for first.

Plainly stated

What this site does not claim

Every one of these would be easy to write and none of them would survive a buyer's audit. Leaving them out is the point.

  • No "leading" or "number one"
  • No zero-defect claims
  • No client logo wall
  • No stock photography
  • No unverified testimonials
  • No zero-discharge claim
  • No 100% quality claim
  • No round-the-clock promise

A buyer in this trade can tell within about a minute whether a supplier's website was written by someone who has stood on a dye house floor. The tells are specific: capacity without a machine list, a certificate without a number, a lead time without the stage it starts from, a gallery that is obviously stock, and a superlative where a measurement should be.

This site is written the other way round. Where we do not have the number, it says so. Where the honest answer is that you should use a different unit, the capacity page and dyeing page say so.

Verify us

You should not have to take any of this on trust. Here is how to check it, and it costs you nothing:

  • Ask for a certificate number and check it with the issuing body.
  • Ask for a video walk of the dye house. We will show the machines we list.
  • Ask us to match a shade you have already approved elsewhere. That is a free test of our lab.
  • Ask for the name of a buyer who has placed a repeat order. We will give it if we have permission to.
  • Ask us a question we would rather not answer. The answer tells you more than the brochure.
