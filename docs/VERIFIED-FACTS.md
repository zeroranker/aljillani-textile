# VERIFIED FACTS — Al-Jilanee Textile Industry (Pvt) Ltd Website

**The evidence register and honesty audit.**

Prepared for three readers: the owner of the company, whoever maintains this website
next year, and anyone who wants to check whether this site tells the truth.

There are three tables in this document.

- **Table 1 — the evidence register.** Every fact the site publishes, or relies on,
  with where it appears, what kind of evidence backs it, and where that evidence came
  from.
- **Table 2 — the honesty audit.** Every claim the site deliberately refuses to make,
  why it refuses, and what the company would need in hand before it could say it.
- **Table 3 — open questions.** Everything still marked UNKNOWN, who answers it, and
  what it blocks.

**One rule governs all three.** If a fact is not in the research document, it is not
published as fact on this site. It is either marked as a placeholder, labelled as an
industry benchmark, or left out. That rule is worth more than any design decision in
the project.

---

## How to read the evidence classes

These four words come from the research document and are used consistently everywhere.

| Class | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **VERIFIED** | The original source was retrieved and read, or the check was run directly. Someone else can repeat it. |
| **MEASURED** | We counted it or calculated it ourselves. Byte sizes, character counts, ratios. Reproducible. |
| **UNVERIFIED** | It sounds right and is widely repeated, but no primary source was reached. Published only where labelled as an industry view. |
| **UNKNOWN** | Nobody could find it. Recorded as a question. Never guessed, never estimated. |

**Where a figure came from matters more than whether it sounds reasonable.** A number
with no source is not a fact, however plausible it looks.

**"Where it appears" uses these values:** the page name and the section or table inside
it; `robots.txt` / `llms.txt` / `sitemap.xml` for the non-page files; `docs/…` for a
document in this project; or **Not published**, when the site deliberately does not
carry the figure.

**"Date checked"** is the date the evidence was gathered. Research figures were checked
in the August 2026 research pass, except the recycled-standards line, which was
checked in September 2026. Build measurements were taken from the delivered draft.

# Table 2 — The honesty audit

**This is the table a sceptical buyer should read first**, and the one a future
maintainer should read before they "just add a quick claim to fill the gap".

Every row is a claim that would have been easy to write, would have looked
professional, and would not have survived a buyer's audit.

| Claim deliberately not made | Why not | What the company would need before it could say it |
|---|---|---|
| **"EOE-CLT" or any "certified as export-oriented unit" claim** | This is a customs and tax status. It is not a marketing phrase, it is checked, and getting it wrong is a legal exposure rather than a credibility one. | **Duty drawback eligibility** | Duty drawback is restricted to value-added products. ## What this document is for

Three uses, in order of importance.

**1. For the owner.** Everything the site claims about your business is in Table 3.
Answer those questions and the placeholders disappear. Everything the site will
*not* claim is in Table 2, with the reason and the evidence you would need to change
that. If you were told a website "needs" those claims to look professional, this table
is the answer.

**2. For a sceptical buyer or investor.** Start at Table 2 and work backwards. The site
publishes what it can stand behind, marks what it cannot, and names the gaps. The
single most useful thing you can do is pick any row in Table 3 and ask the question
out loud. The response tells you more than the website does.

**3. For whoever maintains this site next.** The rule that keeps it honest is one line:

> **If you cannot point to the source, it does not go on the page.**

Before you add a number, a badge, a name or a photograph, check it against Table 1.
If it is not there, it is UNKNOWN, and an UNKNOWN goes on the page as a visible
placeholder or it does not go on the page at all.

**Maintenance checklist** — run this whenever you change anything factual:

- [ ] Can I point to the source for every number I just added?
- [ ] Is it VERIFIED, MEASURED or UNVERIFIED — and does the page say which?
- [ ] If UNVERIFIED, is it labelled as an industry view rather than as our figure?
- [ ] Does every duration name the stage its clock starts from?
- [ ] Does every machine name appear on a nameplate you have photographed?
- [ ] Does every certificate row carry a number, a body and an expiry date?
- [ ] Is the draft ribbon still visible for anything still marked TO CONFIRM?
- [ ] Have I added a claim that Table 2 says we refuse to make? If the evidence has
      genuinely changed, update Table 2 first — do not quietly edit the page.

**One closing note.** The research behind this site had real limits. General web search
was largely unavailable throughout, and one search engine returned provably wrong
results. That is why some rows above read UNVERIFIED rather than VERIFIED. Those rows
are not oversights — they are the record of where the evidence genuinely stops, and
they are the reason a buyer can trust the rows that are marked VERIFIED.

**#74 is the most damaging defect found in these three cycles.** A buyer
fills nine fields describing the fabric, the quantity, the shade and the date,
presses send, and a popup blocker quietly refuses the window. The site says
it worked and empties the form. Several minutes of work, gone, with no copy to

locked-down office networks, which is exactly where an exporter evaluating a
supplier would be.

The fix is three separate decisions, because any one of them alone would still
lose the enquiry:

1. Check what window.open returns and never claim success unless a window
   actually opened.
2. Never call orm.reset() on a path that did not deliver.
3. On a block, insert a real link the buyer clicks themselves. A popup blocker
   cannot stop a genuine user click, so this route always works. Focus is
   moved to it, and the message says plainly that nothing was sent and nothing
   was lost.

opened.
