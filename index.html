What "pure processing" means here

Yarn and greige are our raw materials, not our products. We do not compete with the mills that make them. If you are comparing a processing quote against a composite mill's price, you are not comparing the same thing. And we will say so before you send us the order.

Lead time

Which stage does the clock start from?

"45 days" and "45 days from shade approval" are different promises. Only one of them can be kept. Here is the whole sequence, and exactly where the waiting happens.

  1. Enquiry and route proposal

    You send a construction, a quantity in metres and kilograms, and a target shade. We reply with a processing route, a lot structure, and the stages we think are required, not just the cheapest one.

    Where the clock is running: not yet

  2. Lab dip or strike-off

    A small-batch trial on your actual substrate, matched under controlled light. We submit shade options; you choose or reject. Rejected rounds restart this step, not the next one.

    Where the clock is running: our production time

  3. Your approval

    The clock stops. This is normally the longest single step and it sits in your inbox, not ours. We will ask for a decision within a stated window rather than leaving a lot idle.

    Where the clock is running: your decision

  4. Bulk production

    Every shade is a separate dye lot. Three colourways are three lots, not one, and the last lot to finish, not the first, sets the date. We will tell you which lot is the critical path.

    Where the clock is running: machine schedule

  5. Finishing, inspection and packing

    Stenter and any finishes, then inspection against the retained approved sample, then folding, rolling and labelling. Bulk is never released without inspection against that sample.

    Where the clock is running: our finishing floor

Starting an enquiry

Send us these six things

A processing quote needs more than "how much per kilo". Send what is on this list and you will get a real number back, not a range.

  1. Construction or reference: fabric type, blend, GSM, width, knit or woven.
  2. Quantity: in metres and kilograms, and how many colourways.
  3. Target shade: a physical standard, a Pantone or TCX code, or a photo with the light it was taken in.
  4. Process route: what you need done, and what you have already had done.
  5. Target date: worked backwards from your delivery date, not forwards from today.
  6. Destination and certification: which market it ships to, and any certificate your buyer will demand.
